H-ASIZH: A Highly Reliable Digital Izhikevich
Neuron Core Using Adaptive Threshold Control for
Fault Accommodation
Ali Roshani, Mohsen Ansari, and Mohammad T. Manzuri Shalmani

Abstract—Spiking neural nets scale to microwatts by encoding
information in the timing of events but silicon makes them
vulnerable to permanent stuck-at faults that trap a neuron in
either a firing infinity (digital seizure) or a zero and TMR solves
that at 3.40× the cells. This work pushes fault tolerance inside
the neuron: H-ASIZH (Homeostatic Affine Scaled Izhikevich)
makes the firing threshold a slow state variable whose negative
feedback accommodates a permanent fault without an outside
observer and a Lyapunov argument guarantees that the mean
firing rate is uniformly ultimately bounded against a persistent
fault current. 1-cycle Q7.19 core of the placed and routed layout
of the SkyWater 130 nm process costs 1.39× the cells at 0.813 pJ
per neuron update and brings the dangerous defect set down from
46.2% to 17.5%. Hardening its control plane with a triplicated
restoring organ leaves 4.2% of defects dangerous against 12.0%
for triplication, at 32% fewer cells, at the same energy per update,
and at a 4.76× reliability figure of merit (RFoM) against 1.13×.
Index Terms—Spiking neural networks, neuromorphic computing, fault accommodation, fault tolerance, homeostasis, Izhikevich model, Lyapunov stability, gate-level fault injection, energyefficient VLSI.

I. I NTRODUCTION
HE brain-computer interface and neuroprosthetic devices
must compute within the body. Doing so requires working
under a thermal budget that a standard accelerator cannot
meet, and a means of communicating with tissue that already
communicates in spikes. Neuromorphic computing accomplishes both simultaneously. Encoding information in the time
of events rather than the strength of continuous activations
reduces the volume of data that must be moved, enabling lowmilliwatt operation while still capturing much of the behaviour
of biological circuits [1], [2]; the same behaviour properties
also make neuromorphic computing useful for edge-AI workloads that have surpassed von Neumann architectures [3].
Of all the models for digital emulation, a Izhikevich neuron [4] is an unusually good deal: over twenty firing modes
from two differential equations for a minuscule fraction of the
cost of biophysically detailed models. Multiplierless incarnations are under a thousand transistors [5], [6]; million-neuron
fabrics have been fabricated in silicon [7], [8], however none
of this settles the question: How does the fabric behave as its
transistors die?

Permanent defects are the concern. Manufacturing defects,
electromigration, dielectric breakdown, hot-carrier injection
and radiation damage cause a node to short to ground (SA0) or
to the supply (SA1) [9]. In a spiking neuron the two polarities
fail quite differently. An SA1 in the membrane datapath,
or an SA1 in the threshold comparator, acts as a persistent
parasitic current: every cycle it drives the membrane to the
rail, and it locks the cell into sustained discharge—a digital
seizure that saturates the spike fabric and flattens an implanted
battery in seconds. An SA0, on the other hand, takes out the
drive and silences the cell [10]. The difference is important.
Saturation almost always kills the network, while silencing is
often absorbed in a hidden layer and becomes deadly only at
the output [11], [12]. Defect tolerance in VLSI is not new [13],
but in its traditional incarnation it assumes a datapath whose
correctness is binary—not one that outputs a rhythm.
A. Limits of Conventional and Bio-Inspired Mitigation
The textbook answer is spatial redundancy. Triple Modular
Redundancy (TMR) replicates the module to be protected and
masks faults with majority voting [14]. Inserting TMR into
a neuromorphic substrate it is self-defeating: area and power
at least triple, the voter shoves jitter down a channel whose
information is in its timing, and tripling the circuit triples the
cross-section available to the provoking event [15].
A second line of work has moved towards biological compensation. SPANNER deploys astrocyte-like monitors alongside the cores and modulates synaptic release probability on
detection [16]; some distribute astrocytes to restore individual
firing rates [17], or make that compensation tunable at run
time and report 94% accuracy with 15% of cores faulty [18].
Closest in spirit to this paper, Johnson et al. use homeostatic regulation to provide fault tolerance, but the underlying
mechanism derives from a distributed astrocyte net rather
than directly from the neuron’s own state [19]. Others restore
execution within an already-faulty accelerator [20], or seek
structural containment within a single dendritic branch [21],
or within a single spiking pixel [22]. Two more approaches
complete the list: temporal redundancy swaps replication for
re-execution [23], and mapping schemes move the computation itself either away from an unreliable memory [24], or
between diverse heterogeneous cores [25].

C. Proposed Solution and Contributions
We close all six gaps with a solitary architectural commitment: the fault-tolerance is implemented as part of the
neuron’s own state equations. The neuron we propose, HASIZH (Homeostatic Affine Scaled Izhikevich), is an intrinsic
controller, which treats the firing threshold as a slow state
variable that can be elevated to absorb the energy of a permanent fault. No external guardian is left to break, because the
regulator and control plant are the same circuit, and because
nothing migrates, the fabric remains as deterministic when
faulty as it was when not.
• Reformulation and stability proof. An affine map
carries the Izhikevich equations into a compact, shiftonly domain with no approximation, reproducing nine
firing patterns to within 10−13 RMSE. With the controller embedded in the mapped dynamics, we prove by
Lyapunov analysis that the mean firing rate is Uniformly
Ultimately Bounded under any persistent fault current,
the residual band being set by the spike penalty rather
than by the defect amplitude. Boundedness of the rate and
boundedness of the machine word are distinct guarantees:
the theorem supplies the first, the clamping network of
Section IV-C the second.

IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON CIRCUITS AND SYSTEMS–I: REGULAR PAPERS, VOL. XX, NO. XX, MONTH 2026

Hardware-aware coefficient optimisation. Multiobjective design space exploration binds the controller
parameters to an exact power of two, which simplifies
the resilience arithmetic to a shift-and-add.
• RTL micro-architecture. A 1-cycle unrolled Q7.19
datapath with the 4-segment piecewise-linear estimator
(implemented without multiplier), a clamping network,
a fast-bleed recovery path, and a proportional β-brake mitigation performed entirely in the neuron core, leaving
routing and schedule untouched.
• Physically faithful fault analysis. A technology-agnostic
gate-level injection framework applied to the synthesised
netlist using four-valued logic that compiles the fault into

open frameworks do [44], with an exhaustive campaign
of five architectures. The resulting projection reveals the
proposed core outperforming full triplication by more
than a factor of 4 at one-third less silicon.1

D. The Intrinsic Homeostatic Controller
Prior designs rely on extrinsic supervisors—modelled astrocytes monitoring activity and modulating weights [16]—or
relocate computation away from a damaged core [39], presuming healthy spare capacity. Our route is control-theoretic.
Fault-tolerant control distinguishes masking, which conceals
a defect by replication, from accommodation, in which the
regulator concedes the defect is permanent and retunes a
slow set-point under output feedback until the loop settles
on a new admissible equilibrium. The only quantity inside
a neuron that directly gates the regulated output is the firing
threshold, so we make it a slow state variable θ(t) resting at
θ0 = 1.1 (≡ +5.4 mV)—on the regenerative upstroke, just
below the divergent PWL segment, while the apex +30 mV
(≡ 1.484375) remains the saturation clamp.

B. Phase-Space Interpretation
The mechanism admits a geometric reading in
(Vhw , Uhw , θ). Fault-free, θ stays at θ0 and the state is
confined to a planar slice, executing a canonical limit cycle.
A stuck-at-1 defect on a high-order accumulator bit warps
that vector field; in an unprotected core it destroys the limit
cycle and the trajectory accelerates into saturation. H-ASIZH
prevents the collapse by making θ an active third dimension:
the defect-induced burst activates the penalty term, θ expands
along that axis and lifts the trajectory out of the compromised
plane, deforming the integration boundary and absorbing the
injected energy, while the β-brake deepens the reset along
Uhw and adds damping. Instead of diverging, the trajectory
settles into a bounded three-dimensional limit cycle—an
adaptive homeostatic attractor (Fig. 4).

A. The Fixed-Point Word and Its Guard Bits
The datapath carries a 26-bit signed word in Q7.19 format—
one sign bit, six integer bits, nineteen fractional bits (Fig. 5).
Two consequences of the affine map make that word sufficient
where floating point would otherwise be required.
The first is headroom. With σ = 2−6 , Vhw occupies
[−0.5, 1.5] nominally and is clamped at 4.0, Uhw is bounded
by ±4.0 and θ spans 1.1 to 8.0, whereas Q7.19 represents
±64: every state variable lives within roughly one eighth of the
available magnitude. This matters because two’s-complement
overflow inverts rather than saturates, turning a membrane on
the verge of a spike into one in deep hyperpolarisation in a
single clock edge and voiding the argument of Section III-A.

D. Control-Plane Hardening: Light TMR and the Restoring
Organ
Accommodation defends the wide arithmetic well, because
a defect there perturbs a quantity the loop measures and can
absorb. It cannot defend the loop’s own decision logic: a
stuck-at on the spike comparator, a clamp flag or THETAREG corrupts the signal the feedback is computed from,
and no threshold elevation recovers a decision never taken.
Two variants therefore harden the control plane and leave the
datapath to the controller.
Light TMR triplicates the decision logic only. THETA-REG
becomes three 26-bit registers reduced by a bitwise majority
voter, θv = (θA ∧θB )∨(θB ∧θC )∨(θA ∧θC ), and seven singlebit decisions—the spike flag, brake-enable, fast-bleed enable
and four saturation flags—are triplicated the same way. Each
replica derives its flag from its own threshold copy, so a defect
in one register is outvoted rather than propagated, while VREG, U-REG, the PWL estimator and both adder trees remain
simplex. The cost is 1 500 cells and a 3.6% longer critical path.
The restoring organ closes the gap Light TMR leaves: a
single voter reduces three replicas to one net, and that net is
a single point of failure downstream of everything it protects.
Following the classical construction, this variant triplicates the
voters themselves, so no reduction to a single net occurs before
the register boundary. The 124 cells it adds—about 3%—
remove the last unreplicated decision node, and Section VII-B
shows what they buy.
V. A G ENERIC G ATE -L EVEL FAULT I NJECTION
F RAMEWORK
The stability argument of Section III-A is valid for a
continuous disturbance on an idealised state; silicon does not
break that way. A permanent defect damages a single node of
a synthesised netlist, and whether it reaches the spike output
depends on Boolean structure, on what state the neuron is in,
and how many cycles pass before it propagates. To see what
the architecture can withstand, we must gate-level inject under
a realistic workload.
A. Rationale and Toolchain
Two standard approaches were dismissed. Behavioural injection into a source RTL variable operates at the wrong level:
the synthesis restructures the arithmetic aggressively, so a 26bit adder becomes a carry chain with no counterpart in the

8

source, and the gate-level masking that decides survival is invisible above the netlist. Simulator-resident FORCE/RELEASE
strikes the right level but does not scale well, and is vendorspecific. Open frameworks [44] pursue the bottleneck with
hyperscale compute, but injection remains external to the
design; we inject internally.
Since the framework compiles the fault into the circuit
itself, there are no interventions at run-time. The framework
is implemented with open tooling in five stages (Fig. 7). Each
core is elaborated by GHDL and synthesised by Yosys, with
the circuit fully flattened so all nets are addressable, then
mapped to generic primitives instead of a vendor library, so
the reported distribution remains a property of the architecture.
In addition, a parser maps each primitive output to one of four
semantic zones, namely STATE _ MEMORY, CONTROL _ PLANE
for the nets originated by the threshold comparison or spike
signal, DATAPATH for the wide arithmetics, and COMB _ CLOUD
for everything else. This is not for aesthetic reasons: a datapath
defect affects a quantity the loop tracks and can compensate,
whereas a control-plane defect destroys a signal the loop
depends on.
B. Saboteur Weaving by Logic Shadow Hook
Instrumentation simplifies the engineering burden for a
comprehensive campaign: the instrumentor modifies the netlist
only once rather than once per fault. At every target the
instrumentor inserts a controllable bypass through a three-port
fault bus (fi_enable, fi_target_id, fi_value). Each
target net is disconnected from its primitive driver, its outputs
switched to a shadow net, and the net is re-specified as a
continuous assignment selecting between the two:
assign net = (fi_enable && fi_target_id==ID)
? fi_value : hook_net;

Three properties follow. If fi_enable is low then the
expression is just the original driver so the instrumented netlist
is functionally identical to the golden netlist, and only one
design serves the entire campaign. The fi_target_id is
a top-level input not a constant therefore optimisation cannot
remove the multiplexer - a silent failure mode that would make
naive netlist mutation fail. And fi_value is four state so the
hook can force x or z as easily as a logic level.
C. Simulation and Neuro-Metric Classification
The instrumented netlist is simulated using Icarus Verilog,
driven from Python using Cocotb: the latter’s interface supports clocks, stimulus and sampling, but it is not the injection
mechanism, which is a structural feature. We use a four-valued
IEEE 1364 logic and explicitly simulate the unknown state
propagation - it’s important when a defect feeds a comparator
or select line, as a two-valued simulator would silently select a
branch and produce a more optimistic result than the hardware
delivers. Each run is fault free for 2000 cycles to give the
state time to reach its limit cycle, then the fault is asserted
permanently, running to cycle 10 000 - a timescale made
necessary by the 64-cycle leak constant.
A spiking neuron cannot be graded pass or fail: a train

D. Fault Model and Scope
The campaign applies the permanent single stuck-at model
to all extracted sites, in both polarities, with no fault collapsing. Permanent faults are more difficult for an adaptive
scheme, because a stuck-at node influences all later evaluations, rather than one update [37]. Three limits guide the
numbers. The data reflect coverage under a given workload,
not ATPG fault efficiency [28], and the only observation point
is the spike output, so a defect leaving an unchanged emitted
train counts as masked. The 10−13 residual of Section II-C is
the error in the affine map, not of the implemented datapath,

the stability theorem of Section III-A models a defect as a
current to the membrane equation, covering DATAPATH and

computed from - the gap the hardened variants of Section IV-D
exist to close.

VI. C ASE S TUDY: O N -C HIP L EARNING
The homeostatic machinery which renders H-ASIZH faulttolerant intervenes exactly on the state variables that synaptic
learning would normally alter, so a core hardened in this
manner could have sacrificed the plasticity that learning would
require. This section settles that question - a confidence check
rather than a second contribution.
Fig. 8 shows the system. A 256 → 1024 → 10 network is
built entirely from H-ASIZH cores: a hidden layer trained by
unsupervised non-causal STDP under first-K temporal k-WTA
(K = 3), and a ten-neuron output layer trained by a delta rule
with teacher annealing. Weights are 4-bit; MNIST digits are
reduced to 16×16 and encoded as time-to-first-spike (TTFS).
The network is time-multiplexed onto two physical cores, so
neuron instances account for 4800 cells while 2.26 Mib of
memory dominates the rest, and the mitigation of Section II-D
is carried at negligible system cost: the two hardened cores
draw 182.8 µW together, or 0.177 µW per logical neuron. The
organisation cuts both ways, and the trade should be stated:
amortising one core over hundreds of virtual neurons amortises
the protection, but a single permanent defect in that core then
reaches every neuron mapped onto it—which is precisely why
the core rather than the array is the right unit to harden.
One point is worth making. Most on-chip learning demonstrations so far have resorted to rate coding or rate-like spikedriven rules [45], and a rate code does not care about small
errors in the timing. Temporal coding does care: a TTFS code
encodes everything in the order of the spikes, so any change in
latency corrupts the code directly - which is why an adaptivethreshold neuron is the right substrate for it. We are not aware
of a prior fully end-to-end synthesisable demonstration that
combines TTFS coding with on-chip unsupervised learning
and a fault-accommodating neuron. The problem is simplified,
and is not offered as a benchmark result.
This scope should not be exaggerated. We did not run—
and we don’t claim to have run—a network-fault campaign: a
spiking accelerator is heterogeneous—weight and state mem-

B. Reliability and the Cost Trade-off
Fig. 9(a) shows the results of the distribution of outcomes,
with every node injected in both polarities. The unprotected
core defines the magnitude of the problem: 45.5% of faults
go out of the admissible envelope, while 0.7% are fatal, and
zero outcomes are tolerant (without a self adaptive threshold,

a fault is either totally invisible or destructive, and graceful
degradation is not something the architecture has).
The controller changes the shape of that distribution rather
than shifting it. Critical outcomes fall threefold to 15.3% and
a tolerant population of 20.8% appears where none existed,
the accommodation the Lyapunov analysis predicts. The same
data expose its limit: fatal outcomes rise from 0.7% to 2.2%,
because sustained discharge accumulates inhibitory charge
through the brake, which relaxes only with the intrinsic time
constant—a controller able to suppress a seizure can, by the
same action, suffocate the cell. The restoring organ addresses
exactly that: critical falls to 3.2%, fatal returns to 1.0%, and
masked reaches 79.5%, for 124 cells over light TMR.

triplication, with 32% fewer cells, 22% less power and 36%

does nothing about a defect all three copies see equally,
expands the circuit, but accommodation recomposes a steady
state in the presence of the defect, regardless of how many
copies observe it. The same reading separates this work from
its bio-inspired forebears. The SPANNER [16] and astrocytic
cluster [17], [18] monitors are placed next to the neuron, built
from the same gates on the same die, such that a defect in
the monitor itself removes or reverses the protection. Closest
to this work, Johnson et al. [19] achieve homeostasis from
another rule-holder, a distributed set of astrocytic cells, so
regulator and process do not share the same circuit. Here,
the regulated variable, sensor and actuator are the same three
registers the neuron already needs, so no supervisory fabric is
left to fail. The dendritic family [21] acts inside the neuron
as well, but is resilient structurally at overhead almost 60%,
against 39% here without replicas; migration schemes [24],

VIII. C ONCLUSION
This paper set out to obtain fault tolerance for a digital
Izhikevich neuron at minimal overhead, and did so by moving
the mechanism inside the neuron: the firing threshold becomes
a slow state variable whose negative feedback accommodates a
permanent defect, proven Uniformly Ultimately Bounded and
realised in shifts and additions on a single-cycle Q7.19 datapath at 30.7–91.4 µW. An exhaustive gate-level campaign leaves
4.2% of faults dangerous against 12.0% for full triplication,
at 32% fewer cells, a 4.76× reliability figure of merit against
1.13×, and—the comparison that carries the argument—the
same 1.47 pJ per neuron update. Triplication buys nothing that
accommodation does not buy more cheaply, because every
replica that lowers the vulnerability factor enlarges the target
that multiplies it.
In another way, this mechanism is cheaper than we’d
thought. The threshold-adapting neuron can’t runaway or be
permanently outcompeted, so the three registers that absorb
the defect also suppress neural death and dominance in unsupervised learning: 1020 of the 1024 hidden neurons remain
active through training. This isn’t a fault-tolerance result; it’s
due to the learning dynamics, and we report it as such, but
the silicon bought for high-reliability is active even without a
fault.
The way forward is composition rather than extension.
Intrinsic accommodation and system-level protection are complementary: the former eliminates the neuron as a single point
of failure, ECC-protected memories, control-path duplication
and checkpoint-and-retry address the parts of an accelerator
that a neuron model cannot reach. Extending the treatment
to the memory hierarchy, the arbitration logic and the control
